Japanese toilets

Dryer (乾燥)

Also called: 乾燥 (kansō), 温風乾燥 (onpū kansō), warm air dryer, DRYER, dry

WTF it means

Blows warm air from under the back of the seat to dry your bottom after a wash. Yes, really.

What to do about it

Press it while you're still sitting, and 止 (stop) when you've had enough. It's in no hurry, so most people pat dry with paper first (or instead). On some seats pressing it again changes the air temperature.

For the record

It isn't a hand dryer or a fan for the room. Like the washes it only works while you're sitting, and it switches itself off after a few minutes.
