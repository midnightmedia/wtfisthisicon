Medicine and medical symbols

Pill packs, prescriptions & first aid. 26 of them, decoded.

Crossed-out torn box (don't use if package is damaged)

Don't use the product if its packaging is torn, punctured, open or wet.

Sort it soon

Black triangle ▼ (additional monitoring)

The medicine is being watched extra closely for side effects, usually because it's new to the market or it's a biological medicine (such as some vaccines).

P or POM in a box (UK medicine categories)

The medicine's UK legal category.

Rx only (US prescription symbol)

℞ is the old symbol for a prescription, from the Latin 'recipe', meaning 'take'.

Pregnant woman crossed out (pregnancy warning)

The medicine can harm an unborn baby.

Car in a triangle (may affect driving, France)

The medicine can affect your driving (drowsiness, blurred vision, slower reactions).

Days and arrows on a pill pack

Calendar packs, used mostly for the contraceptive pill, print the days of the week and arrows on the foil so you take the pills in order and can see at a glance whether you've taken today's.

Solid factory (manufacturer)

The name and address next to the filled-in factory belong to the manufacturer: the company legally responsible for the product.

Outline factory (date of manufacture)

The date next to the outline (not filled-in) factory is when the product was made.

Hourglass (use-by date)

Use-by date: don't use the product after the date printed next to the hourglass.

LOT (batch number)

LOT in a box marks the batch code: which production batch the item came from.

Crossed-out 2 (single use only)

Single use: use it once, then throw it away.

Triangle with exclamation mark (caution)

Caution: there are important warnings or precautions in the instructions that don't fit on the label, such as who shouldn't use it, or what could go wrong (or give a false result) if it's used incorrectly.

STERILE (and STERILE EO, R or A)

The contents were sterilised (made free of germs) and stay sterile until the pack is opened or damaged.

Thermometer with two lines (temperature limit)

Store (and transport) it between the two temperatures printed at the ends of the lines: the lower one is the minimum, the upper one the maximum.

LATEX in a rounded triangle (contains natural rubber latex)

The product or its packaging contains natural rubber latex, which can cause allergic reactions, occasionally severe, in people allergic to it.

MD in a box (medical device)

The product is a medical device rather than a medicine.

Σ with a number (number of tests in the box)

Contains enough for that many tests: Σ 7 means there's enough in the box for seven tests.

C and T lines on a test

C is the control line: it shows the test has worked.

Person in a box (type BF applied part)

An electrical safety rating.

MR in a triangle, square or crossed circle (MRI safety)

MRI scanner safety symbols, found on implant cards, implant leaflets and medical equipment.

Crossed-out glass (don't drink alcohol)

Don't drink alcohol while taking this medicine.

Closed eye or Zzz (may cause drowsiness)

The medicine may make you sleepy or less alert.

White cross on green (first aid)

First aid: a first-aid kit, first-aid room or first aider is here.

Heart with a lightning bolt (defibrillator / AED)

An automated external defibrillator (AED, or defib) is here: a portable machine that gives an electric shock to get the heart of someone in cardiac arrest (collapsed, unresponsive and not breathing normally) beating normally again.

Biohazard symbol (sharps and clinical waste)

Biological hazard: it contains, or may contain, something that can spread infection, such as used needles, blood or body fluids, or lab samples.
