Charger and plug symbols

The tiny print on power bricks. 17 of them, decoded.

Double insulated (square in a square)

Class II, or double insulated: the live parts are wrapped in two layers of insulation (or one extra-strong layer), so the device doesn't need an earth connection to be safe.

Earth / ground (protective earth)

Marks the earth (ground) connection.

For indoor use only (house)

The device is designed to be used indoors only.

Efficiency level (Roman numeral in a circle)

Shows how energy-efficient an external power supply is, under the International Efficiency Marking Protocol.

AC: alternating current (wavy line)

The wavy line means alternating current: the kind of electricity that comes out of a wall socket.

DC: direct current (line over dashed line)

The line over a dashed line means direct current: the steady, one-direction electricity that batteries and gadgets run on.

Polarity (centre-positive / centre-negative)

Shows the polarity of a round barrel-style DC plug.

Fuse (rectangle with a line through it)

Marks a fuse: a thin wire that heroically melts and cuts the power if too much current flows, to prevent a fire.

Safety isolating transformer (two circles in a shield)

The charger contains a safety isolating transformer: the dangerous mains side is completely separated from the low-voltage output, so the end you touch (the USB socket, cable or connector) can't become live even if something fails.

Read the instructions (open book)

There's important information in the instructions or safety leaflet, usually about how to use or connect the product safely.

Dangerous voltage (lightning bolt in a triangle)

Warning: dangerous voltage inside, enough to give a serious electric shock.

Hot surface

Warning: this surface gets hot enough to burn you during normal use.

RCM mark (tick in a circle, in a triangle)

The Regulatory Compliance Mark: the product meets Australian and New Zealand rules for electrical safety and radio interference.

FCC logo

The US Federal Communications Commission's mark: the device has been checked so its radio interference stays within US limits, so it won't mess up radios, Wi-Fi or TV.

UL Listed (UL in a circle)

UL (originally Underwriters Laboratories), an independent testing lab, has tested samples of this product against US safety standards for fire and electric shock.

CCC mark (China)

China Compulsory Certification: the product meets China's mandatory safety rules and can legally be sold in mainland China.

PSE mark (Japan)

Japan's compulsory electrical safety mark.
