Chargers & plugs

DC: direct current (line over dashed line)

Also called: direct current, DC, ⎓, IEC 60417-5031

WTF it means

The line over a dashed line means direct current: the steady, one-direction electricity that batteries and gadgets run on. On a charger it's usually next to the OUTPUT, e.g. '5V ⎓ 2A' means 5 volts DC at up to 2 amps.

What to do about it

When swapping a power supply (for a router, speaker, etc.), the replacement must have the SAME output voltage and polarity, and an amp (A) rating that's the same or higher. More amps is fine, because the device only takes what it needs. The wrong voltage can damage it.

For the record

USB-C chargers list several outputs, like 5V⎓3A / 9V⎓3A / 20V⎓3.25A. They agree the right one with the gadget automatically, which is why one USB-C charger can safely power lots of different devices.
